Caeseura: Designing a Custom 30% Wireless Split

Building a compact, split, open source row-staggered board with no experience.

March 12, 2026 My First Custom!

The Caeseura is a custom row-staggered 30% split design built completely from scratch by me. To be honest my main goal was just to create a board that worked. I wanted to add all the cool features... Hence why there is choc v1 and v2, and MX compatiblity, along with the nice!nano v2 as the MCU of choice. I also wanted to do something that I had not seen a lot in other's builds, row-staggered-ness.

I had the idea for Caeseura ever since I built my first split keyb, corne kit from Typeractive (write up here). Coming from an engineering background, and wanting to create something that was truly mine, I set out in search of help. I found the Ergogen (absolem) discord a great place to bounce ideas off of people, and get help when I was stuck. The Ergogen tool was fundamental in creating this dream of mine, even if it is not designed for row-staggered keyboards. Sorry for the link spam, but FlatFootFox's guide to ergogen was a lifesaver for this project, and to community in general. I would recommend giving all his keyboard blogs a read, but the ergogen guide series (5ish parts and counting) is really helpful. Even now, as I am writing this about a little under a year into the custom ergo keyboard design hobby, I still have to search through the guide every once in a while. Mainly for yaml syntax things, because with power comes great... debugging strange behaviors.

Some context to where I was at in my engineering journey. I had messed around with CAD software for basic 3D modeling, but had never made anything to 3D print, or for any functional purposes, where tolerances and measurements were a must. So safe to say I was jumping in the deep end, somewhat without any swimming practice. This is why it took about 3 months for the design to finally come to a point where I was happy with it enough to send it off to be fabricated (JLCPCB).

Designing a board this small coming from barely using a corne, and liking a 75% keyb, came with some unique layout challenges. Here were some of the goals:

  • Arrow Keys Built In: Allowing for easy use for multitudes of applications, namely rotting away on YouTube, and navigating code and video timelines.
  • Split Design: Because it "supposedly good for you" and its what all the cool kids were doing.
  • Multi Switch Compatibility: Full support for MX & Choc v1/v2 switches because you never know when you might want to switch it up a little.
  • Wireless: I fell in love with the tri-state meta of most full-ish size mechanical keybs, and wanted no cables to fluff up my life. Also it made it so I could travel with it easier!
  • Open Source: Fully available for the anyone, if they were completely out of their mind, to build it themselves.

The workflow that I mainly used to get this keyboard off to the printers was, ergogen -> kicad pcb. And then for the firmware it was Nick Coutso's keymap layout tools (import kicad pcb) -> Genteure's ZMK shield wizard -> Nick Coutso's keymap editor (links below). One of the boards that was helpful for creating the row-staggered design in ergogen's mainly columnar focused ecosystem was FFF's 65 Crimes. I may or may not've taken the exact spacing for the left side outer column.
